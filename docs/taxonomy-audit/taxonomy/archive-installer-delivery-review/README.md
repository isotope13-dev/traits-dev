# Archive and installer delivery review

Archive-carried obfuscated VBScript, remote MSHTA shortcuts, and browser-icon
shortcut lures move with their supported concealment, execution, or
masquerading subjects. Installer pages move to execution lures where their
deceptive context is established; a bare-IP download alone stays a suspicious
download capability.

The ActiveX/onload/LoadModule combination is a suspicious invocation clue,
not proof that the handler activates the control. A VSIX with an installer
URL and process launch is a suspicious payload-execution clue, without a
claim that the URL was downloaded. PowerShell download, elevation and Run-key
evidence moves with persistence clues; the embedded PE download/path match
does not establish execution and remains a suspicious download capability.
The former dropper ancestor consumer retains the moved findings explicitly.
