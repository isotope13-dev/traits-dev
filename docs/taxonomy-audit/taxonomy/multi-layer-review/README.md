# Multi-layer review

XOR wording beside Base64 evidence is a notable text profile, not a hostile
obfuscation finding. PHP self-reading and XOR text with Base64 is a suspicious
transformation profile; the separate Base64/eval and Base64/ROT13/exec text
patterns retain their respective evaluation and command subjects. Their
unreferenced any-of grouping duplicated the two individual findings and was
removed.

Java string fragments and ProcessBuilder reflection text are observations;
their combination does not prove the reflected method was invoked. Ruby's
bound Base64/XOR/eval syntax retains its script-evaluation objective.

The 72 encoding profiles and 88 class-reflection rules were split by their
observed subjects. Existing parent-directory selectors remain recursive, and
references to individual rules were updated.

Shell decode pipelines, character decoding, environment exports and Base64
input encoding now live with those operations. The combination of multiple
string-reconstruction techniques remains a suspicious concealment objective;
Base64 input, positional exec and a stage filename alone form a suspicious
profile rather than proof of a self-packed payload. The Ruby XOR/inflate/eval
chain and the injected remote-script finding remain execution objectives.

The 70-rule Base64 encoding leaf and 74-rule string-reconstruction leaf were
split by subject and mechanism. Parent selectors preserve their existing
members; two positive Base64-directory consumers additionally include the new
SELF-variable input encoder. Individual references were updated.

The macOS rules combine sparse strings and section measurements with shell
references, repeated `popen` text, or fork/session API references. Those are
suspicious profiles, not proof of a backdoor or persistence. Their underlying
section-entropy and string-density observations moved to metadata. Apple
signature exclusions were preserved; excluding an Apple signature alone does
not mean a binary is unsigned.

Whitespace/XOR concealment remains an encoding objective, and the hidden
HTML constructor chain remains an execution objective. A generic any-of
obfuscation grouping used by a shortcut-hijack finding was renamed to
describe its actual builder or state evidence; it does not itself establish a
supply-chain attack. Its existing consumer was updated.

Python's hex-escaped names are encoded name observations, and reflection or
builtins reassignment alongside them does not by itself establish a loader.
Those findings now describe their actual clues. Combinations of XOR techniques
remain concealment evidence, while decoded evaluation chains follow their
execution sink. Compressed marshal materialization is a reconstruction
finding; its existing bytecode-write consumer remains a separate staging
objective. Existing matches, exclusions and consumers were retained, with
criticality reduced where the previous claim exceeded the evidence.

JavaScript's Base64-like shard arrays and fragmented `fromCharCode` XOR
operations now follow their observed encoding and decoding subjects. SVG
constructor-property, encoded-string, shard-array and script-host combinations
are profiles where the predicates do not establish an injected script or
loader. The .NET runtime basename remains a software-path observation.

PowerShell's Boolean-masked injection, XOR/eval chains and reflected assembly
entrypoint findings now follow their respective execution sinks. Base64 or
hex plus XOR and ScriptBlock construction identifies construction, not by
itself invocation; those and the upload, rotation and compression combinations
now describe their actual clues. Previous broader hostile claims were reduced
where their conditions did not establish the claimed execution or C2.

The remaining VBScript rules have moved by subject. Large Base64-like table
entries and repeated literal arguments are encoding observations; partial
crypto names and encoded assembly references are fragments, not proof of a
specific decryptor. Their quoted concatenation requirement is retained to
avoid matching ordinary words in JavaScript source maps. Environment-spliced
batch strings and combined XOR/COM/name obfuscation describe concealment.
Decoded evaluation, batch execution and embedded PE or ActiveX launch clues
follow their execution sinks. Other collections of crypto fragments, window
state, environment reads and generic Run calls describe their actual evidence
without claiming a PowerShell payload or environment-based staging.

The multi-layer directory is now empty. References and existing exclusions
were updated or preserved as appropriate.

Nearby name-fragmentation, batch execution and embedded script-host leaves
were approaching the future limit. Name splitting, PowerShell launch chains,
and WMI launch chains now have subject-specific sibling categories. No
exact directory selectors depended on the previous leaves; individual
references were updated.
The name-splitting review also corrected a rule that inferred a LOLBin launch
from name fragments and module access alone; those clues remain suspicious,
and its package-level consumer now describes the install-hook and DLL clues
without claiming a sideload.
