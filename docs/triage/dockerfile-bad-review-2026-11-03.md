# Dockerfile mislabeled-bad review — 2026-11-03

The 35 snapshot artifacts include standalone Dockerfiles and source archives. The files were scanned with cache disabled in Cleave and Atom and inspected for actual behavior. Results: 1 bad, 12 good, 15 dual-use, and 7 inconclusive.

`59237eb64414` remains bad. Its embedded `index.js` collects hostname, user, home, DNS and package information, attempts to read `/etc/passwd` and `/etc/shadow`, and posts those values to an out-of-band endpoint. Both scanners report hostile host-profile and account-data upload traits. An apparent proof-of-concept context does not make this behavior benign.

Fifteen artifacts are dual-use. `1fc6244b3a72`, `82840298c389`, `c857c4f963aa`, `a2e2241cf5d9`, `6fa065531ec8`, `e568a7f9a6de`, `a9647bdfd618`, `4d613b6b0f89`, `1aa4b08d310e`, `961a40c78298`, and `9d47fbe71580` are recipes for C2, agent, or security-framework builds; their source payloads and deployment context are not established by the recipes. `78803a399a1a`, `b1a7b013eb03`, and `3505e7554b6d` define explicitly marked exploit-lab infrastructure. `41a5a0a5ade1` is a security-analysis project archive with active malicious Office documents in its test corpus; the nested files account for its hostile static findings. Its presence should not be mistaken for proof that the surrounding analysis application executes those documents against a victim.

Twelve are good: `97c1a65fd586` installs a kernel-development toolchain and headers; `6bcfdfa9a9ac`, `44ccee9b3b9c`, `72a649778658`, `59f167d39e23`, `dd8248a01c46`, `3c7cb7d45fdb`, `64ec07dfc786`, `00cc92586d91`, `dc350940ed9f`, `687159aba0c2`, and `39a9937d8c67` contain development, infrastructure, or security-tool source and templates. The source archives were scanned and their package structure and representative metadata inspected. In `39a9937d8c67`, the reported no-import EFI objects are embedded in OVMF firmware images; the generic EFI rule was lowered to suspicious, and both scanners then cleared the hostile root findings.

Seven are inconclusive: `c2cccda453fc` names a malware service but does not include its application code; `bd9a5c1b8c38` enables root SSH with a known password, an unsafe configuration whose intended environment is unknown; `84ffd6ec2112` builds a named Apache backdoor from absent source; `2f3fa8c01eb2` copies an absent pause binary; `3737047514a7` copies an absent Nginx module and config; `0e012eadb208` silently downloads and runs an opaque remote helper; and `da0023ba3b51` is a very large workflow-automation source archive not fully characterized here.

Generic Dockerfile rules for framework builds, exploit labs, hidden opaque downloads, root SSH, and replacing a server module or pause binary were reduced to suspicious. These observations do not establish the payload's behavior or unauthorized use. Both scanners were rerun on representative affected standalone recipes and cleared the unsupported hostile findings. The out-of-band host-data POST sample above still reports hostile after the changes. Full `make validate` passed, including corrected suspicious fixtures for the opaque helper downloader.
