A3CE1F2AD378: StormAttack native botnet DLL installer/injector.
Rizin: 0x401260 constructs %SystemRoot%\\System32\\StormServer.dll, opens
HKLM\\SYSTEM\\CurrentControlSet\\Services\\LanmanServer\\Parameters,
deletes ServiceDLL and writes the substituted DLL path as REG_EXPAND_SZ.
0x401150 launches a target process, allocates remote memory, writes the DLL
path and uses GetProcAddress(LoadLibraryA) as the remote-thread entrypoint.
The resource at RVA 0x5060 is a 39936-byte PE carrying Storm ddos DNS,
Welcome to use storm ddos, and STORM:%d|%s|%s|%s|%s protocol fingerprints.
The Microsoft certificate digest does not match this image; this proves
integrity failure, not Microsoft authorship or the time of modification.
Move the generic service-DLL hijack to service/loader, update its consumer,
and require service registry evidence and resource/file-write capabilities
in the family injector rule. Two hostile findings on the outer artifact.

1BE30C4745F0: native Protected Storage credential stealer/autorun worm.
Populated UPX0 and resolved 0x7c... function pointers suggest a memory dump
or modified UPX image, rather than a normal intact packed executable.
Rizin: 0x42a4d4 decodes pstorec.dll using the XOR-2 helper 0x428653,
resolves PStoreCreateInstance, traverses COM storage objects and emits
username/password records through 0x42a48e. 0x42964e formats the complete
HTTP form POST request. The autorun writer around 0x42a34e decodes
[autorun] and ShellExecute=autorun.exe and writes both to autorun.inf.
This establishes credential acquisition, outbound credential-report capability
and self-propagation. No evidence supports the collector's WannaCry label.
Clarify descriptions and move the literal driver path out of kernel-module
capabilities into fs/path/system, preserving its consumers and scope.
Three hostile YAML behaviors; a fourth hostile finding is Cleave's built-in
UPX decompression failure. The failure is reproduced with upx -t, but its
hostile criticality is an engine policy and cannot be corrected in YAML.

C393C3096C96: custom .NET ransomware and input/file stealer.
Decoded CIL (dnfile/dncil) confirms Main invokes persistence, keylogging,
security disabling, replication and BypassUAC, and schedules worker tasks.
RealEncryption enumerates Desktop\\TestFolder recursively, reads files,
EncryptAES transforms their bytes, writes the suffixed ciphertext and deletes
originals. This scope is deliberately limited; do not describe full-drive
ransom encryption. AES uses a fixed key/IV. Simulation labels coexist with
real malicious activity and are not a benign exclusion.
StartKeylogger's worker loops over virtual keys 0..255, GetAsyncKeyState,
and AppendAllText. ContinuousDataExfiltration reads KEYLOG_FILE and CLIP_FILE,
labels the payloads keylog/clipboard, then passes them to SendToC2. That
routine serializes and sends form content through HttpClient.PostAsync to
http://vaoroizgg.cc. Screenshots and other files are also collected.
BypassUAC creates the per-user ms-settings shell open-command key, writes the
current executable and empty DelegateExecute, then launches fodhelper.exe.
Add the plain-label polling/log-write/POST profile; retain the original
colon-label rule and avoid duplicate findings. Move the Run-key/key-polling/
Taskmgr policy profile from hook to polling. Describe shadow-delete command
references as static evidence rather than observed execution. Three hostile
findings in input exfiltration, ransom encryption and UAC bypass.
